Cataract: When Is It Time to Consider Surgery?

1 September 20266 min readAditya Kamaraja Hospitals

Cataract is one of the most common reasons people over fifty visit an eye clinic, and one of the most treatable. Because it develops gradually, many people live with it for years without realising how much their vision has changed. Understanding what a cataract is, and what it does to everyday sight, makes it much easier to decide when to act.

What is a cataract?

Inside each eye is a natural lens that focuses light onto the retina, in the same way a camera lens focuses light onto film. This lens is normally clear. A cataract is a clouding of that lens. As it becomes cloudier, less light passes through cleanly, and vision becomes dull, hazy or blurred.

Cataract is most often a natural part of ageing, although it can also be linked to diabetes, long-term use of certain medicines, an eye injury, or, less commonly, be present from birth. It is not a growth or a film over the eye, and it does not spread from one eye to the other, although both eyes are often affected in time.

Symptoms people usually notice first

The early signs are easy to mistake for ordinary ageing or a spectacle change: vision that seems cloudy or misty, colours that look faded or yellowish, increased glare from sunlight or oncoming headlights, halos around lights at night, needing brighter light to read, and spectacle prescriptions that keep changing without giving clear vision. Some people find that near vision temporarily improves before it worsens again.

Cataract does not usually cause pain or redness. If you have those symptoms, they need to be examined for other reasons.

Does every cataract need surgery straight away?

No. In the early stages, a change of spectacles and better lighting are often enough, and your ophthalmologist may simply review you periodically. Cataract surgery is the only way to remove a cataract, but the timing is a decision you make together with your doctor, based on how much the cataract is affecting your life rather than on a number on a chart.

When it is time to consider surgery

The practical question is this: is the cataract stopping you from doing the things you want to do safely and comfortably? Many people decide it is time when they find it hard to drive, especially at night; when reading, watching television or recognising faces has become a strain; when glare makes going out in bright sunlight unpleasant; when new spectacles no longer help; or when the cataract makes it difficult for the doctor to examine and manage another eye condition, such as diabetic changes in the retina.

If any of these sounds familiar, it is worth a consultation. The examination confirms whether a cataract is the cause of the change, rules out other causes, and gives you a clear picture of your options.

What to expect from a cataract consultation

At the consultation, your vision is measured, the lens and the back of the eye are examined, and your general health and any medicines are reviewed. If surgery is advised, your doctor will explain the procedure, the choice of lens options and what recovery involves, so that you can decide without pressure.

At Aditya Kamaraja Eye Clinic, cataract surgery is performed as a modern, small-incision procedure with a choice of lens options, and most patients go home the same day. Your ophthalmologist will discuss what is suitable for your eyes.

In short

A cataract is not an emergency, but it is not something to simply endure either. If cloudy vision, glare or fading colours are getting in the way of daily life, a consultation will tell you where you stand and what your options are. That conversation, rather than the calendar, is what decides the right time.

This article is for general health information only. It does not replace a consultation with a qualified healthcare professional, and it is not a diagnosis or personalised medical advice. If you have a health concern, please speak to a doctor.
