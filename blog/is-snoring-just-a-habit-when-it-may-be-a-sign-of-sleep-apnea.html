Is Snoring Just a Habit? When It May Be a Sign of Sleep Apnea

28 August 20266 min readAditya Kamaraja Hospitals

Snoring is so common that most families treat it as a joke or a mild nuisance. Often that is all it is. But for some people, snoring is the sound of breathing that is being partly blocked during sleep, and it comes with pauses in breathing that neither the sleeper nor the family may have noticed. This is why it helps to know the difference between ordinary snoring and the pattern that may point to sleep apnea.

Why people snore

When we fall asleep, the muscles of the throat relax and the airway narrows a little. If air has to squeeze through a narrower space, the soft tissues vibrate, and that vibration is snoring. Sleeping on the back, a blocked nose, a cold, alcohol in the evening, extra weight around the neck and the natural changes of ageing can all make it louder. Snoring on its own, with refreshing sleep and no other symptoms, is usually not a medical problem.

What is sleep apnea?

Sleep apnea is a condition in which breathing repeatedly pauses or becomes very shallow during sleep because the airway closes for a few seconds at a time. Each pause briefly disturbs sleep, usually without the person waking fully. Because these interruptions can happen many times a night, sleep stops being restful, even after eight hours in bed. Only a proper evaluation can confirm whether someone has sleep apnea; snoring alone does not.

Signs that snoring may be more than a habit

A few patterns are worth taking seriously. Loud snoring that is broken by silences and then a gasp, snort or choking sound, often noticed first by a partner. Waking unrefreshed even after a full night, or waking with a dry mouth or a dull headache. Feeling very sleepy through the day, nodding off while reading, watching television or, more worryingly, while driving. Difficulty concentrating, irritability or low mood that has no other explanation. Waking often at night to pass urine.

Having one of these does not mean you have sleep apnea. Having several, especially together with loud snoring, is a good reason to have your sleep looked at.

Why it is worth getting evaluated

Sleep-related breathing problems affect more than the night. Poor sleep makes it harder to manage weight, blood pressure and blood sugar, and daytime sleepiness affects safety at work and on the road. The good news is that sleep-related breathing problems are treatable, and many people notice a real difference in their energy and wellbeing once the cause has been identified and managed.

What a sleep consultation involves

A consultation starts with a conversation: your sleep pattern, your symptoms, what your family has observed, and your general health. Your doctor may then recommend an assessment of your sleep to understand what is disturbing it. From there, the care is planned around what is found rather than a standard package.

At Aditya Kamaraja Hospitals, Integrated Sleep Care brings medical evaluation, sleep assessment and personalised care together in one place, with a pulmonologist and sleep physician on the team. Walk-ins are welcome, and online consultations are available for sleep-related concerns if you would prefer to start from home.

In short

If your snoring is loud, broken by pauses, and you never feel properly rested, treat it as a health question rather than a family joke. A sleep consultation is a simple first step, and it often brings answers that the whole household has been waiting for.
